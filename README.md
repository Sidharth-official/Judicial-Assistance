# Judicial Drafting Assistant
## User Guide for Judicial Officers of the District Courts of Himachal Pradesh

*Prepared for the use of all Hon'ble Judicial Officers serving in the District Courts of Himachal Pradesh — including Civil Judges (Junior & Senior Division), Judicial Magistrates, Chief Judicial Magistrates, Additional District & Sessions Judges, and District & Sessions Judges — and their court establishment*

---

> **A Note on Tone**
> This guide is written for all Hon'ble Judicial Officers of the District Courts of Himachal Pradesh who will use this system in their chambers — whether presiding over civil, criminal, or magisterial jurisdiction. It deliberately avoids technical language. Where a technical concept must be introduced, it is explained through an analogy drawn from courtroom practice.

## 1. Purpose & Ethical Boundaries

### What This System Is

The **Judicial Drafting Assistant** is a private, offline drafting and reference tool designed exclusively to support the Hon'ble Judicial Officer in the mechanical and clerical aspects of judgment and order writing. Its function is analogous to that of a highly organised **private drafting clerk** — one who has read every Bare Act on the shelf, has indexed every precedent in the court's library, and can, upon instruction, produce a well-structured first draft of a finding or order paragraph.

Just as a senior drafting clerk assembles the relevant sections, cites the applicable case law, and structures the language in the Judge's known style — but never substitutes his own judgment for that of the Court — this system does exactly the same, and nothing more.

The system assists the Hon'ble Judicial Officer in:

- **Transcribing reasoning** already arrived at into formal, structured legal prose.
- **Structuring findings** under the correct heads and sub-heads of a judgment.
- **Citing governing statutory provisions and precedents** so that the officer may then verify each citation before signing.
- **Maintaining stylistic consistency** across a large volume of daily orders and judgments.

---

### What This System Is Not

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This system does **not** decide cases. It does **not** determine Points for Determination. It does **not** evaluate the credibility of witnesses. It does **not** weigh evidence.

The Supreme Court of India, through its guidelines on the use of Artificial Intelligence in the judicial domain, has unambiguously held that the judicial mind — the application of reason, conscience, and law to the facts of a cause — is an exclusively human function vested by the Constitution in the judicial officer. No technology may substitute for, or be allowed to influence, that function.

This system is built on that understanding. The Hon'ble Officer always provides:
- The **facts** as found after appreciation of evidence.
- The **Point for Determination** as framed by the court.
- The **Finding** — the conclusion on that point — as arrived at by the officer's independent judicial reasoning.

The system then drafts the *expression* of that finding in formal language. The **judicial officer remains the sole arbiter of fact and law.** The draft produced is a working document and acquires no legal force unless and until reviewed, corrected, and signed by the officer.

---

### 100% Confidentiality & Data Sovereignty

One of the most sensitive obligations of a judicial office is the protection of information shared by litigants, witnesses, and counsel within the proceedings. Depositions, chamber deliberations, bail applications, and evidence records are matters of the highest confidentiality.

> [!CAUTION]
> Many commercially available AI tools — such as those accessible through a web browser — transmit everything typed into them to servers located abroad, operated by private corporations, beyond the jurisdiction of Indian law. **Using such tools for court work would constitute a grave breach of confidentiality.**

This system operates on an entirely different principle:

- **It runs entirely inside your laptop.** Nothing is sent over the internet.
- **It requires no internet connection** to function. The Wi-Fi may be switched off during use, if the officer so chooses.
- **No data ever leaves the chamber.** Case facts, names of parties, findings, and evidence summaries typed into this system remain exclusively on the officer's machine.
- **No commercial company has access** to any information processed by this system.

Think of it as the difference between dictating to a clerk in your own chamber versus dictating over a telephone line that is tapped by a stranger. This system is the former.

## 2. Hardware Specifications — In Plain Terms

This section describes the two types of machines involved in operating this system. The distinction is important: one machine is used daily in the chamber; the other is used only occasionally, by a technical assistant, to update the law library.

---

### The Chamber Laptop — *Where the Daily Work Happens*

This is the Hon'ble Officer's own laptop or the court-issued laptop kept on the officer's desk.

**Minimum requirements (in plain language):**

| What to Check | Minimum Needed | Where to Find It |
|---|---|---|
| RAM (Memory) | 8 GB | Control Panel → System → About |
| Processor | Intel Core i5 (or equivalent AMD/ARM) | Same as above |
| Storage Space | 10 GB free on the hard disk | File Explorer → This PC |
| Graphics Card | **Not required.** Any standard laptop will do. | — |
| Internet Connection | **Not required** during use | — |
| Operating System | Windows 10, Windows 11, or Linux | System → About |

> [!NOTE]
> Unlike many AI tools that require expensive graphics processors (often associated with gaming computers), this system runs entirely on the ordinary CPU (the main chip) of a standard government-issue or personal laptop. No special hardware purchase is required.

The reason this is possible is that the AI model installed is specifically selected for its small size — it has been deliberately compressed to run efficiently on modest hardware without compromising the quality of its legal drafting output.

---

### The Research Station — *Where the Law Library Is Built and Updated*

This is a separate, more capable laptop or desktop computer, typically maintained by the technical assistant, Reader, Bench Clerk, or Ahlmad in the court establishment, as designated by the Hon'ble Officer. It is used only when:

- The system is being set up for the first time, or
- The digital law library is being updated with new Acts or judgments (described in Section 4 below).

**Minimum requirements:**

| What to Check | Minimum Needed |
|---|---|
| RAM (Memory) | 12 GB or more |
| Processor | Intel Core i7 or equivalent |
| Storage Space | 20 GB free |
| Internet Connection | Required once, during initial setup only |

Once the law library has been built on the Research Station, it is copied onto a USB drive and transferred to the Chamber Laptop. After that transfer, the Chamber Laptop operates entirely independently, with no further need for the Research Station or any internet connection.

## 3. Daily Workflow — How to Use the System

The following steps describe the Hon'ble Officer's typical interaction with the system on any given working day. The entire process — from opening the application to receiving a draft — ordinarily takes between two and four minutes, depending on the length and complexity of the matter.

---

### Step 1 — Open the Application

**Double-click the shortcut labelled "Judicial Drafting Assistant" on the desktop.**

A window will open in your web browser (the same programme used to browse the internet, though no internet is actually used here). The application interface will appear, divided into two halves:
- The **left half** is where you provide instructions.
- The **right half** is where the drafted text appears.

> [!NOTE]
> If the browser does not open automatically within thirty seconds, inform your technical assistant. The system may need to be started once using a separate shortcut labelled "Start Server" — this is a one-time step at the beginning of each working day.

---

### Step 2 — Enter the Case Information

You will see three clearly labelled text fields on the left side of the screen. Enter the following:

---

**Field A — Case Facts & Evidence**

In this box, type a brief summary of the material facts as found by the court after appreciation of evidence. This need not be exhaustive — a clear, concise summary of the relevant facts that bear on the Point for Determination is sufficient.

*Example:*
> *The complainant, PW-1, deposed that on the night of 14 March 2024, the accused entered the premises. The accused's presence was corroborated by CCTV footage (Exhibit P-3) and the testimony of PW-2. The defence pleaded alibi but failed to produce the witness cited in the written statement.*

---

**Field B — Point for Determination / Specific Issue**

This is the precise legal question that the court has framed and which is being decided by this particular finding or paragraph. Enter it exactly as it has been framed in the case.

*Example:*
> *Whether the prosecution has proved beyond reasonable doubt that the accused committed the offence of house-trespass with intent to commit theft, punishable under Section 457 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860?*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This field is the most important one for the system to function well. The system uses the Point for Determination as its search query to locate the most relevant governing statute, controlling precedent, and stylistic reference from the law library. A precisely framed Point yields a precisely drafted result.

---

**Field C — Judge's Finding & Reasoning Notes**

In this box, enter the officer's own conclusion on the Point and the key reasons for that conclusion, even in brief notes or bullet points. The system will expand these notes into formal judicial prose.

*Example:*
> *- Prosecution case proved beyond reasonable doubt.*
> *- PW-1 and PW-2 are consistent and credible. Minor contradictions do not dent their testimony on the core issue.*
> *- Alibi defence has been disbelieved for failure to examine the alibi witness.*
> *- Conviction recorded.*

---

### Step 3 — Generate the Draft

**Click the button labelled "⚖️ Generate Judicial Draft".**

The system will briefly display a message indicating that it is working. During this time, it performs two tasks invisibly and automatically:

1. **It consults the Digital Law Library** — acting as a reference librarian who pulls the most relevant statute, the most on-point Supreme Court or High Court precedent, and an example of the drafting style that the court itself has used in past judgments.

2. **It instructs the private drafting clerk** — passing all of the above, together with your entries, to the AI model, which then composes the draft finding.

---

### Step 4 — What the System Produces

Once complete, the draft text will appear on the right side of the screen. It will typically include:

- A formal recitation of the charge or issue.
- A structured discussion of the relevant provision of law (the governing Section from the Bare Act).
- A reference to the controlling judicial precedent on the point (from the Supreme Court or the High Court).
- The finding itself, incorporating the reasons entered in Field C, expressed in formal judicial language.
- The dispositif (operative conclusion): conviction/acquittal, or order as the case may be.

Below the draft, the system will also list the **three sources it consulted**:

| Source Type | What It Contains |
|---|---|
| 📜 Statutory Law | The specific provision of the Bare Act governing the offence or issue |
| 🏛️ Binding Precedent | A judgment of the Supreme Court or the HP High Court on the point |
| ✍️ Style Reference | An example of how this court has drafted similar findings in past judgments |

---

### Step 5 — Review, Verify, Edit, and Export

> [!CAUTION]
> **The draft is a starting point, not a finished product.** The Hon'ble Officer must read the entire draft carefully before it is incorporated into any signed document.

Specifically, the officer should:

1. **Verify every cited provision.** Confirm that the Section cited is the correct one, that it is still in force, and that it applies to the facts as found. (See Section 5 on the important distinction between IPC/CrPC and BNS/BNSS.)

2. **Verify every case law citation.** Check that the case exists, that the ratio as described is accurate, and that it has not been overruled or distinguished by a later decision.

3. **Edit the language** to reflect the officer's own voice, style, and any nuance not captured in the draft.

4. **Copy the final text** into the court's official order/judgment template in Microsoft Word, or paste it into the existing document being prepared.

The system does not directly export to Word, but any text on screen may be selected, copied (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C), and pasted (Ctrl+V) into any word processor.

## 4. Updating the Digital Law Library

### The Analogy of the Physical Law Library

Imagine a well-maintained chamber library containing a complete set of the Bare Acts and a curated collection of important judgments. When Parliament enacts a new law or amendment, a physical copy is added to the shelf. When a landmark judgment is delivered, a copy is filed in the relevant section. The library's utility is proportional to how current and well-organised its collection is.

The Digital Law Library in this system works on exactly the same principle. The law library is a collection of folders on the Research Station:

```
legal_corpus/
├── bare_acts/          ← Acts of Parliament and State Legislature (PDFs/TXTs)
├── supreme_court/      ← Supreme Court judgments on relevant points
├── hp_high_court/      ← HP High Court judgments and circulars
└── past_judgments/     ← Selected past judgments of this court, for drafting style
```

The AI model itself **never needs to be retrained or reinstalled**. Updating the law library is as simple as adding a new file to the correct folder and running one automated update command. This is by design — the knowledge of the system is held in the library, not baked into the model.

---

### Protocol for Adding New Laws or Judgments

The following three steps constitute the complete update procedure. Steps 1 and 2 are performed on the **Research Station** by the designated technical assistant, Reader, Bench Clerk, or Ahlmad. Step 3 is the transfer to the Chamber Laptop.

---

**Step 1 — Place the Document in the Correct Folder**

Save the new document (PDF or TXT format) into the appropriate subfolder:

| Type of Document | Folder to Use |
|---|---|
| New Bare Act or Amendment (e.g., BNS 2023, BNSS 2023, BSA 2023) | `bare_acts/` |
| Supreme Court judgment | `supreme_court/` |
| HP High Court judgment or circular | `hp_high_court/` |
| A well-drafted judgment of this court, for style reference | `past_judgments/` |

> [!TIP]
> Use clear, descriptive file names. For example: `BNS_2023_Full_Text.pdf`, `Suo_Motu_PIL_Bail_Conditions_HP_HC_2025.pdf`, or `IPC_1860_Consolidated.txt`. The filename will appear as the **citation reference** in the system, so a descriptive name makes verification easier for the Hon'ble Officer.

---

**Step 2 — Run the Update Script**

Ask the technical assistant to open the command terminal and type the following command, then press Enter:

```
python ingest.py
```

This command instructs the system to:
- Read all the documents now in the library folders.
- Process and index them (this takes between two and fifteen minutes depending on the number of documents added).
- Store the updated index in the database folder (`legal_kb_lancedb/`).

The technical assistant will see a progress report on screen. When the message **"✓ Ingestion complete. Knowledge base is ready."** appears, the update is finished.

---

**Step 3 — Transfer to the Chamber Laptop via USB**

1. Copy the updated database folder (`legal_kb_lancedb/`) onto a USB drive.
2. On the Chamber Laptop, replace the old `legal_kb_lancedb/` folder with the new one from the USB drive.
3. Restart the Judicial Drafting Assistant application.

The Chamber Laptop will now have access to the updated library. **No internet connection is required at any stage of this process.**

> [!NOTE]
> If the Chamber Laptop and Research Station are the same machine (i.e., the update is being run on the judge's own laptop), Steps 2 and 3 are combined — running the update script is sufficient, with no USB transfer needed.

## 5. Judicial Caution & Best Practices

The following practices are strongly recommended to ensure that the tool is used responsibly, in a manner consistent with the dignity of the judicial office and applicable institutional directives.

---

### A — Mandatory Verification of Every Citation

This is the single most important caution in this guide.

The system retrieves citations from the Digital Law Library that has been built and maintained locally. Its accuracy is therefore dependent on:
- The accuracy and completeness of the documents placed in the library, and
- The accuracy of the source documents themselves.

> [!WARNING]
> A system can only cite what it has been given. If a document in the library contains a typographical error, an incomplete provision, or an outdated version of a Section, the system may reproduce that error in its draft. The Hon'ble Officer's review is the final and definitive quality check.

**Before signing any order or judgment:**

- Locate and open the physical or official digital copy of the cited statute (e.g., on the India Code portal, accessed separately with internet connection or from an offline copy).
- Read the cited paragraph of the case law in its original reported form (SCC, AIR, or the court's official website).
- Confirm that the citation is accurate, in force, and applicable.

---

### B — The Critical Distinction: IPC/CrPC versus BNS/BNSS

With the coming into force of the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)**, the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**, and the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)** with effect from **1 July 2024**, there now exist two parallel criminal codes governing different cases simultaneously.

The governing principle is as follows:

| Date of Cause of Action / FIR | Applicable Code |
|---|---|
| On or before 30 June 2024 | IPC, 1860 / CrPC, 1973 / Indian Evidence Act, 1872 |
| On or after 1 July 2024 | BNS, 2023 / BNSS, 2023 / BSA, 2023 |

> [!CAUTION]
> The system will retrieve the most semantically similar provision from its library. If both the old and new codes are present in the library, it may retrieve either, depending on how the Point for Determination is phrased. The Hon'ble Officer must always confirm that the provision cited corresponds to the code applicable to the specific case before the court, determined by the date of the cause of action or lodging of the FIR.

It is recommended that when framing the Point for Determination in Field B, the officer specify the applicable code: *e.g., "...punishable under Section 103 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023"* rather than *"...under the relevant law"*. This specificity will guide the system toward the correct provision.

---

### C — Log of AI Assistance

Institutional practices regarding the disclosure and recording of AI-assisted drafting are evolving. The High Court of Himachal Pradesh and the Supreme Court of India may, from time to time, issue administrative or practice directions on this subject.

It is recommended that the Hon'ble Officer:

1. **Maintain a chamber register** (or a digital note) recording, for each matter in which this tool is used: the case number, the date of use, and a brief note that AI-assisted drafting was employed for first-draft preparation.

2. **Review any circulars or directions** issued by the High Court Registry or the Supreme Court e-Committee on the subject of AI tools in judicial work, and ensure compliance with any disclosure or annotation requirements prescribed therein.

3. Be prepared, if so directed by an appellate court, to clarify that the final judgment represents the independent judicial reasoning of the officer, with AI assistance limited to drafting expression.

---

### D — The System Is a Tool, Not a Precedent

The draft produced by this system is generated text. It has no legal standing of its own. It does not constitute a judgment, a finding, or a citation merely by virtue of being produced. It becomes part of the judicial record only when the Hon'ble Officer has reviewed it, accepted it (in whole or in part), edited it as appropriate, and signed the final document.

The analogy that bears repeating: a skilled drafting clerk may produce an excellent first draft of an order. The order becomes the order of the court only when the Judge signs it. Until then, it is paper.
